# 02-founder-strategy-under-constraint

## Topic

Should we keep pushing our AI startup through a narrow enterprise wedge, or admit we are drifting into consulting?
## Context

~~~text
We are a two-person startup with about six months of runway. I am the technical founder; my cofounder handles sales and customer discovery. We built an AI workflow tool for small accounting firms. The product reads messy client documents, extracts missing fields, drafts follow-up emails, and prepares internal review notes.

The demo works well. Accountants immediately understand the pain. But conversion is weak. We have talked to around 40 firms, run 11 demos, and only three are paying. Current revenue is about $2,400 MRR. Each customer saves time, but onboarding is painful because every firm has different folder structures, document naming habits, and legacy software.

The tension:

1. If we keep selling to accounting firms, we may learn a real niche and build a defensible workflow product.
2. If we keep customizing integrations, we may become a services shop with AI branding.
3. If we narrow to one painful workflow, the market may be too small or too slow.
4. If we pivot horizontal, we may lose the only concrete customer pain we understand.

My cofounder wants to chase a bigger market while investor interest in AI automation is still hot. I think we have not earned the right to generalize yet. We are also tired: support requests interrupt product work, and every sales call produces a slightly different requested feature.

The current options are:

1. Keep selling to accounting firms and build integrations.
2. Narrow down to one painful workflow and make it self-serve.
3. Pivot to a horizontal AI automation product for any small business.
4. Pause sales for a month and manually study existing customer work until we find a repeatable wedge.

We need a decision that preserves survival, avoids fake progress, and does not trap us in consulting.
~~~
