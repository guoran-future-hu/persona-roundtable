# 08-community-project-strategy

## Topic

Open-source project strategy: should we build a community or accept institutional backing?
## Context

~~~text
I maintain a small open-source project that helps people run local AI roundtables. It began as a personal experiment, but a few hundred developers now use it and a volunteer community has formed around it.

A large company has offered funding, cloud credits, and two engineers. In exchange, it wants a formal roadmap, influence over governance, and product features that make the project easier to support for professional users. The offer could accelerate development and make the project sustainable.

The community is divided:

1. Some contributors want to accept the support, improve reliability, and reach a much larger audience.
2. Others fear the project will lose its independence, with decisions increasingly driven by the sponsor’s priorities rather than by users and contributors.
3. The current volunteer process is creative but slow. Decisions are messy, documentation is incomplete, and energy comes in bursts.
4. Rejecting the offer preserves autonomy, but we may lose the chance to build durable infrastructure while interest is high.

I need to decide whether to accept the backing, reject it, or design a third structure such as an independent foundation with clear boundaries. Please analyze the central tradeoff, how to test the choice through practice, where to establish a durable user base, and when cooperation with a powerful organization becomes unhealthy dependence.
~~~
